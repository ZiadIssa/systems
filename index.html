Case study

Taking a 30-year-old store online.

Aladdin Video Games has sold games and electronics for more than 30 years. I built everything they run on now: the online store, the admin panel behind it, and the software at the counter.

30+years in business
4systems built
2languages, Arabic and English
3people run all of it
The starting point

A great store with no way to sell online.

Aladdin is run by a team of three, so whatever I built had to run without anyone babysitting it. Their domain still pointed at an unfinished WordPress site, and its old pages were still the ones showing up on Google.

They needed a store customers could actually buy from, a back office they could manage themselves, and tools for the counter that keep working when the internet doesn't.

What I built

Four systems, one store.

Online store

  • A PC builder for putting together a custom rig
  • Mega-menu, spec filters and search in both languages
  • Dark mode, recently viewed items and install-as-app

Admin panel

  • Products, offers and orders in one place
  • A menus editor that drives the whole navbar
  • Full backups with a selective restore

Desktop ERP

  • Installment sales for bigger purchases
  • A cash drawer counter and scheduled backups
  • Shipped with a full PDF user manual

Point of sale

  • Barcode scanning, discounts and printed receipts
  • End-of-day cash counts and Z-reports
  • Works fully offline, and a crash can't corrupt a sale
Going live

Launch was more than flipping a switch.

  1. 01

    Their own domain

    Moved aladdinvideogames.com onto Cloudflare without breaking the mailboxes they already used.

  2. 02

    Order emails that scale

    Replaced a 200-a-month email service with emails sent from the database: 300 a day for free, in the store's own colours, with product photos.

  3. 03

    Locked down

    Every table locked, the admin password hashed, and every admin change checked by the database itself.

  4. 04

    Found on Google

    New titles, product data with price and stock, a sitemap built from the database and server-rendered shop pages, to replace the old WordPress results.

Since launch

They keep coming back for more.

After launch, Aladdin started paying for new features: a menus tab so they can rebuild the navbar themselves, a three-level category menu with images, and a dark mode that keeps their white-background product photos looking right.

And because it's a games store, there's a playable Pac-Man hidden in the navbar, with a maze shaped like the word ALADDIN.

Point of sale

A till that never loses a sale

Problem

A shop counter can't stop when the internet drops or the power cuts in the middle of a sale.

Fix

The POS keeps running offline and saves every change safely, so a crash can't corrupt the data. End-of-day cash counts show over or short at a glance.

HR system

Check-ins nobody can fake

Problem

Staff could check in for each other, or check in without being at the shop.

Fix

Cake shop

Orders that fit the kitchen

Problem

Every cake is made to order, with flavours, fillings, custom flower colours and prep times of three to five hours or more.

Fix

An order flow that prices every choice as you pick it, and only offers same-day delivery when the kitchen can actually make it.

Online store

A menu that slipped away

Problem

The new three-level category menu kept snapping shut as you moved the mouse towards it, and it worked one time and failed the next.

Fix

Found the cause: the menu was being rebuilt from scratch on every change. Fixed that, and gave it a short grace period so moving diagonally into it feels natural.

Online store

Specs that kept reshuffling

Problem

The client set the order of a product's specifications, saved it, and got them back in a different order.

Fix

Traced it to the database quietly re-sorting them, and changed how they're stored so the order always survives. Proved it on a real database before shipping.

Design

Dark mode with white photos

Problem

The store wanted a dark mode, but every product photo has a white background baked in.

Fix

A dark navy page with the product cards kept white, so every photo still looks right. One rule holds it together instead of hundreds of edits.

Design

A navbar that ran out of room

Problem

When the client added a new menu group, the navbar broke onto a second line, even on a wide screen.

Fix

The links were squeezed into the search bar's width. Freed them to run edge to edge, and made long labels shrink before the main links ever get cut off.

Sharing

Links that looked empty on WhatsApp

Problem

Sharing a product with a customer on WhatsApp showed a plain link with no photo or price.

Fix

Every product page now carries its own preview, so a shared link shows the photo, the name in both languages and the price.

Launch

Moving a domain without losing email

Problem

The store's domain still ran an unfinished WordPress site, and the company's mailboxes lived on it too.

Fix

Moved the domain to Cloudflare with every mailbox kept working, then put the new store and its order emails on the same domain.

Design

The Arabic font that never showed

Problem

Arabic text on the store was quietly falling back to a plain default font on every page.

Fix

Tracked it down to one misplaced line in the stylesheet. Arabic now shows in the font it was designed with.

Store manager

Installments without the paperwork

Problem

A shop that sells on installments needs to know who owes what, when it's due and what's in the drawer.

Fix

A Windows store manager with installment sales, a cash drawer counter for every shift and scheduled backups, shipped with a one-click installer and a printed manual.

Gelateria

New photos without calling a developer

Problem

The gelateria's team adds new photos themselves, and didn't want to wait on code changes every time.

Fix

Built the site to pick up new images on its own. They drop the photo in, and it appears in the right place.

Game

A level for every song

Problem

Hand-charting a rhythm game level for a single song takes hours, so a game like that usually ships with only a few songs.

Fix

Built a beatmap generator for JamJam that listens to any track, finds the beats and the vocals, and charts a playable level in seconds.
